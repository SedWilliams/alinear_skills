# Photosynthesis: source notes

Sample material for trying the skills. Written for this repository from standard introductory biology.

## The big picture

Photosynthesis turns light energy into chemical energy stored in sugar. Overall:

6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6 O2

It runs in two linked stages inside the chloroplast. The light reactions capture energy. The Calvin cycle spends that energy to build sugar from carbon dioxide.

## Light reactions (thylakoid membranes)

- Chlorophyll and other pigments in photosystem II and photosystem I absorb light.
- At photosystem II, water is split. This releases oxygen, protons, and electrons. The oxygen plants release comes from water, not from carbon dioxide.
- Energized electrons pass down an electron transport chain, which pumps protons into the thylakoid space.
- Protons flow back out through ATP synthase, which makes ATP. This is chemiosmosis.
- Photosystem I re-energizes the electrons, which reduce NADP+ to NADPH.

Products sent on to the Calvin cycle: ATP and NADPH. Byproduct: O2.

## Calvin cycle (stroma)

1. Fixation. The enzyme rubisco attaches CO2 to a five-carbon sugar, RuBP. The result splits into two molecules of 3-PGA.
2. Reduction. ATP and NADPH from the light reactions convert 3-PGA into G3P.
3. Regeneration. Most G3P is used, with more ATP, to rebuild RuBP so the cycle can continue.

Three turns fix three CO2 and release one net G3P, using 9 ATP and 6 NADPH. Two G3P make one glucose. The cycle returns ADP, phosphate, and NADP+ to the light reactions.

## How the stages depend on each other

The Calvin cycle doesn't use light directly, but it depends on the ATP and NADPH the light reactions make. In darkness, those run out and the Calvin cycle stops soon after. The light reactions in turn need the ADP and NADP+ the Calvin cycle returns.

## Limiting factors

The rate of photosynthesis is set by whichever factor is in shortest supply: light intensity, CO2 concentration, or temperature. Temperature matters because the Calvin cycle runs on enzymes.

## Photorespiration

Rubisco can also bind O2 instead of CO2. That wastes energy and fixed carbon. It gets worse in hot, dry conditions, when leaves close their stomata to save water: CO2 inside the leaf drops and O2 builds up. C4 and CAM plants have ways of concentrating CO2 around rubisco that reduce photorespiration.

## A common misconception

"Plants photosynthesize instead of respiring" is wrong. Plants carry out cellular respiration in their mitochondria all the time, day and night. Photosynthesis happens only in the light.
